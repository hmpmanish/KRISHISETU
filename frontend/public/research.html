SIH Problem Statement: 26193

1. Problem Statement & Core Need

The Official SIH Mandate

SIH26193 — Student Innovation: Developing solutions, keeping in mind the need to enhance the primary sector of India — Agriculture and to manage and process our agriculture produce.

Our Identified Ground Reality

After harvest, a farmer does not simply ask "What is the price of my crop?". The actual questions are highly fragmented:

  • Should I sell now or wait?
  • If the price will rise, should I store it?
  • If the shelf life is low, what do I do?
  • Can I get more value by processing this crop?
  • Where do I sell it? Is the buyer genuine?
  • If my crop is rapidly spoiling, how do I rescue it?
The Core Problem: Farmers may have agricultural produce, but the decision about what to do with that produce is fragmented across market information, quality, shelf life, storage, processing and buyer trust.

KrishiSetu unites this fragmented information into a single post-harvest decision workflow.

2. Why Post-Harvest Decision Support Matters

Government-backed research has identified post-harvest losses across agricultural commodities. A 2022 NABCONS study commissioned by the Ministry of Food Processing Industries assessed 54 commodities across 15 agro-climatic zones and 292 districts. The reported loss ranges included approximately:

  • Cereals: 3.89–5.92%
  • Pulses: 5.65–6.74%
  • Oilseeds: 2.87–7.51%
  • Fruits: 6.02–15.05%
  • Vegetables: 4.87–11.61%

These figures show that post-harvest management is a genuine agricultural problem, not only a theoretical problem.

Research Implication: The question is not just "How do we increase production?" but rather "How do we make better decisions about produce after it has been harvested?"

3. Existing Solutions vs KrishiSetu

A. e-NAM (National Agriculture Market)

e-NAM is a pan-India electronic trading platform connecting APMC mandis. It focuses on online trading, transparent bidding, and price discovery.

The Gap: e-NAM solves the "Where and at what price can I sell?" problem. KrishiSetu comes before the final market action: "Should I SELL, STORE, PROCESS, or RESCUE this batch?"

B. Agmarknet

Agmarknet is a government portal providing daily market prices and arrivals.

The Gap: It provides raw data, not decisions. A farmer sees "Tomato price is low," but doesn't know if they should store it based on their specific batch's remaining shelf life. KrishiSetu turns Agmarknet data into actionable decisions.

C. Private Agritech Startups (e.g., DeHaat, Ninjacart)

These players focus on supply chain aggregation, acting as middlemen/buyers.

The Gap: They do not provide an independent, agnostic decision support system for the farmer. KrishiSetu empowers the farmer to make the decision themselves, rather than just locking them into a corporate supply chain.

4. Judge FAQs & Show-Stopping Answers

Q1: "Isn't e-NAM already doing this?"
e-NAM is a marketplace for executing a trade. KrishiSetu is a decision engine that happens before the trade. e-NAM helps you sell; KrishiSetu tells you IF you should sell, or if you should process/store it instead based on shelf life and quality.
Q2: "How will a poor farmer use this AI app?"
We designed an "Assisted Access Mode". FPOs (Farmer Producer Organizations), CSCs (Common Service Centres), or mandi facilitators will use this tool on behalf of the farmer. The farmer is the beneficiary, not necessarily the digital operator.
Q3: "How does the Trust & Verify system actually work?"
We don't create our own verification. We act as a "Setu" (Bridge). We pull data via API from MSME Udyam, FSSAI, and e-NAM registries. We provide a 'Proof Vault' where the farmer can see the actual registration certificate of the buyer before trusting them.
Q4: "What happens if the AI gives bad advice?"
Our core philosophy is: "AI Recommends. The Farmer Decides." The AI acts as an advisor, showing the 'Why' and the 'Risks'. The farmer always has the final override authority.

5. The KrishiSetu Logic Map

How our AI Engine routes produce based on real-world constraints:

  • SELL
    Condition: Shelf Life is OK (>5 days), Market Price is High or Trending Up.
    Action: Route to e-NAM or local Mandi.
  • STORE
    Condition: Shelf Life is High (>15 days), Market Price is currently Low.
    Action: Connect to WDRA registered cold storages.
  • PROCESS
    Condition: Crop is suitable for processing (e.g. Tomatoes into puree) AND Market Price for raw crop is Low.
    Action: Connect to FSSAI/MSME registered processing units.
  • RESCUE
    Condition: Shelf Life is Critical (< 2 days) or Spoilage Risk is extremely high.
    Action: Bypass normal market wait times; trigger distress alerts to bulk buyers at discounted rates to prevent 100% loss.
